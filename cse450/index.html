CSE 450 is BYU-Idaho’s applied machine-learning course. Every module hands the team a real dataset and a business question, then grades the answer on a held-out test set the models never trained on.

Team NorthWind (Caleb Dilley, Dallin Wagner, Jonathan Oliphant and Nels Buhrley) worked through four of them in Spring 2026: deciding which bank customers to call, pricing houses, forecasting hourly bike rentals and reading traffic signs. The projects move from scikit-learn classifiers to gradient boosting, neural-network ensembles and convolutional networks trained from scratch on GPUs. Each page below covers the problem, the data, what we tried, the held-out results and what we would do differently.

The projects

  • Module 2 · Classification

    Bank Marketing →

    Choosing which customers to call, scored in dollars instead of accuracy.

    • scikit-learn

  • Module 3 · Regression

    Housing Prices →

    Pricing King County homes with XGBoost and location features.

    R² 0.8787 on the 81-home holdout

    • XGBoost
    • k-means
    • Feature engineering

  • Module 4 · Forecasting

    Bike Demand →

    Forecasting hourly bike-share rentals with a holiday-aware MLP + XGBoost blend.

    RMSE 108.0 rentals/hr, R² 0.912 (384-hour blind window)

    • Keras
    • XGBoost
    • Ensembling

  • Module 5 · Computer vision

    Traffic Signs →

    Classifying 43 kinds of German traffic sign with CNNs trained from scratch.

    99.0% top-1 on a 201-image holdout (2 errors)

    • PyTorch

    • Spatial transformer
